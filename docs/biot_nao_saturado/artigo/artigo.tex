\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================

Intensity--duration thresholds for shallow landslides, $I=aD^b$ with $a$ and $b$ obtained by statistical analyses,
were first proposed by \citet{caine1980} and are used at local, regional and global scales \citep{guzzetti2008}, as
summarized by \citet[p.~2913]{wang2021}. Physically based thresholds come instead from analyses of the slope. In
geotechnical practice the stability of slopes under rainfall is ``often performed using a two-stage method'', a seepage
analysis followed by a stability analysis with the computed pore-water pressures, or with a finite element method with
hydro-mechanical coupling \citep[p.~1]{liu2021}; ``it is common practice to import data from seepage analyses into
stability codes'' \citep[p.~791]{pedroso2015}. \citet[pp.~1534--1535]{rahardjo2007} and \citet[p.~324]{tang2018}
solved the transient seepage with SEEP/W and computed the factor of safety (FS) with SLOPE/W;
\citet[p.~4]{pradhan2022} used a fully coupled flow--deformation analysis in PLAXIS~2D, based on Biot's theory, and the
strength reduction method.

The parametric study of \citet{rahardjo2007} on a homogeneous slope is a reference of the uncoupled approach. They
concluded that the initial water table and the slope geometry ``only played a secondary role'' and that ``for a given
rainfall duration, there was a threshold rainfall intensity which would produce the global minimum factor of safety''
(p.~1532); this threshold intensity $I_{r(\mathrm{thr})}$, defined at the lower inflection point of a sigmoid fitted
to the minimum FS, increases with the saturated permeability $k_s$ (p.~1539); and slopes with $k_s\le10^{-6}$~m/s,
even at $63^\circ$, ``will not fail under short-duration rainfall ($T_r\le24$~h) even when the rainfall intensity is
extremely high'' (p.~1541), because most of the rainwater runs off (p.~1539). Three hypotheses of this approach are
examined here.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Atmospheric pore air.} \citet[p.~811]{sheng2008} take the air pressure at the atmospheric value as
``approximately true for most in situ conditions''; \citet[p.~136]{li1999} and \citet[p.~792]{pedroso2015} adopt it to
simplify the model, and the coupled slope analyses of \citet[p.~3]{wang2018mpm}, \citet[p.~4]{liu2021} and
\citet[p.~4]{pradhan2022} also neglect the air pressure. \citet[p.~292]{fredlund2006} restricts the assumption to
``situations where the soil is exposed to atmospheric pressure and the air phase is continuous''. Comparisons of
passive-air and fully coupled analyses ``are rare'' \citep[p.~4]{ghorbani2018}, and the literature disagrees on
their outcome: \citet[p.~2]{khoei2021} state that the passive-gas assumption leads ``to unrealistic outcomes'',
citing among others \citet{callari2009}, whose passive-air solution of the Liakopoulos drainage test is ``in good
agreement with available experimental data'' \citep[p.~491]{callari2009}.
\item \emph{Uncoupled seepage.} \citet[p.~2]{pradhan2022} report that coupled analyses ``have been reported to provide
a better prediction of factor of safety''; \citet[p.~315]{fredlund2006} notes that ``the uncoupled simultaneous
solution yields satisfactory results for geotechnical engineering purposes''. Neither
quantifies the effect of the coupling on the rainfall that triggers a slope failure.
\item \emph{Flow rule.} The coupled analyses of rainfall-induced slope failure cited above use a dilation angle of
$0.1^\circ$ \citep[p.~6]{wang2018mpm} or zero \citep[p.~5]{liu2021}; \citet[Table~2]{pradhan2022} do not list one.
None of the studies cited here varies the dilatancy.
\end{enumerate}
These studies compare analyses by the minimum FS during and after the rainfall \citep[e.g.][p.~324]{tang2018}.

This paper quantifies how these three hypotheses change the critical rainfall intensity of an unsaturated slope, the
smallest intensity of a rainfall of given duration that brings the slope to failure. A three-field formulation
(displacement, pore-water and pore-air pressures) is implemented in the open-source finite element library NeoPZ
\citep{devloo1997} and verified against the drainage test of \citet{liakopoulos1965}, with the measurements reported
by \citet{callari2009}. The slope of \citet{rahardjo2007} is the reference case: four combinations of coupling and
air model separate the contribution of each hypothesis, and the effect of the dilatancy, of the soil permeability, of
the Bishop parameter, of the confinement of the air and of the mesh is then assessed. Section~\ref{sec:lit} confronts
the results with the statements of the literature listed above.

\section{Formulation and implementation}\label{sec:form}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Governing equations}

The soil is a deformable porous medium with two immiscible fluids, water and air, under isothermal conditions;
grains and water are nearly incompressible and the air is an ideal gas; dissolved air and vapour are neglected.
Small strains and quasi-static equilibrium are assumed. With tension positive and pore pressures positive in
compression, the total stress is
\begin{equation}
\bm\sigma=\bm\sigma'-p_B\,\mathbf I,\qquad p_B=\chi\,p_w+(1-\chi)\,p_a, \label{eq:bishop}
\end{equation}
where $\bm\sigma'$ is Bishop's effective stress \citep{bishop1959} and $\chi$ the Bishop parameter. The unknowns are the
displacement $\mathbf u$ and the gauge pressures $p_w$ and $p_a$; the capillary pressure is $p_c=p_a-p_w$. The
balance equations of momentum, water mass and air mass are
\begin{gather}
\nabla\cdot\bm\sigma-\gamma\,\mathbf e_y=\mathbf 0,\\
\frac{nS_w}{K_w}\,\dot p_w+n\,\dot S_w+S_w\,\dot\varepsilon_v+\nabla\cdot\mathbf q_w=0,\\
\frac{nS_a}{p_a+p_{atm}}\,\dot p_a-n\,\dot S_w+S_a\,\dot\varepsilon_v+\nabla\cdot\mathbf q_a
+\frac{\mathbf q_a\cdot\nabla p_a}{p_a+p_{atm}}=0,
\label{eq:massa}
\end{gather}
with $S_a=1-S_w$, $S_w=S_w(p_c)$, $\varepsilon_v$ the volumetric strain (positive in expansion), $\gamma$ the total
unit weight, $\mathbf e_y$ the upward unit vector, a unit Biot coefficient and Darcy's law
\begin{equation}
\mathbf q_w=-\frac{k_{rw}}{\gamma_w}\,\mathbf K\left(\nabla p_w+\gamma_w\mathbf e_y\right),\qquad
\mathbf q_a=-\frac{k_{ra}}{\gamma_w}\frac{\mu_w}{\mu_a}\,\mathbf K\left(\nabla p_a+\gamma_a\mathbf e_y\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf K$ is the saturated conductivity tensor, $k_{rw}$ and $k_{ra}$ the relative permeabilities and
$\mu_w/\mu_a=55.6$. The air mass balance is the volume balance of the mass equation divided by the air density
$\rho_a\propto p_a+p_{atm}$: its flux term is $\rho_a^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_a\mathbf q_a)=\nabla\cdot\mathbf q_a+
\mathbf q_a\cdot\nabla p_a/(p_a+p_{atm})$. The last term is the term $(\dot{\mathbf w}^g/\rho_g)\cdot\nabla\rho_g$ of
\citet[Eq.~35]{ghorbani2018} and the term $\dot w_{gi}\,\partial\rho_g/\partial x_i/(S_g\rho_g)$ of
\citet[Eq.~36]{li1999}, both obtained from a mass balance with the gas density inside the divergence
\citep[Eq.~33]{li1999}.

As in \citet[pp.~1533--1534]{rahardjo2007}, the retention curve is that of \citet{fredlund1994} with the correction
factor $C(\psi)=1$, $S_w=[\ln(e+(p_c/a)^n)]^{-m}$, and the water permeability is $k_w=k_s\Theta^p$
\citep{leong1997} with $\Theta=\theta_w/\theta_s=S_w$ and $p=4$. The air relative permeability is the Brooks--Corey
form of \citet[Eq.~48]{khoei2021}, $k_{ra}=(1-S_e)^2(1-S_e^{(2+\lambda)/\lambda})$ with $\lambda=3$ and $S_e=S_w$,
bounded below by $k_{ra}^{\min}=10^{-4}$.

The effective stress follows the Mohr--Coulomb criterion with elastic moduli $E$, $\nu$. With an associated flow rule
($\psi=\phi'$) the return mapping is analytical, with the consistent tangent; with $\psi<\phi'$ an iterative
non-associated return mapping is used. The strength envelope $\tau=c'+(\sigma_n-u_a)\tan\phi'+(u_a-u_w)\tan\phi^b$
\citep{fredlund1978}, used by \citet[p.~324]{tang2018}, corresponds to $\chi=\tan\phi^b/\tan\phi'$ on the suction;
\citet[p.~1533]{rahardjo2007} use $\phi^b=\phi'=26^\circ$, i.e.\ $\chi=1$, which is the default here.
\citet[p.~312]{fredlund2006} reports that the shear strength increases with $\phi'$ ``for matric suctions up to the air
entry value of the soil'' and at a decreasing rate beyond it; $\chi=S_e$ is therefore examined in
Section~\ref{sec:gen}.

Four models are compared (Table~\ref{tab:modelos}). In the uncoupled models the terms $S_w\dot\varepsilon_v$ and
$S_a\dot\varepsilon_v$ are removed from Eq.~\eqref{eq:massa}, i.e.\ the flow is solved on a rigid skeleton. In the
passive-air models $p_a=0$ in the whole domain and the air mass balance is not solved. Model (iii) has the
hypotheses of the analyses of \citet{rahardjo2007} and \citet{tang2018}: seepage on a rigid skeleton (SEEP/W) and the
stability computed with the resulting pore-water pressures.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

The rainfall is a flux on the horizontal projection of the ground surface with the unilateral condition $p_w\le0$,
inflow $\le I_r$ and complementarity, solved as an active set of ponded vertices; without rainfall the same condition
is a seepage face. Conditions of this type are discussed by \citet[Section~3]{pedroso2015}, who notes their analogy
with the complementarity conditions of plasticity. \citet[p.~1534]{rahardjo2007} applied ``a boundary flux, $q$,
equal to the desired rainfall intensity'' to the surface of the slope. The pore air is atmospheric on the ground
surface and the base and lateral boundaries are closed to the air (the effect of opening them is examined in
Section~\ref{sec:confinamento}). As in \citet[p.~1534]{rahardjo2007}, the water pressure is hydrostatic on the
lateral boundaries below the water table and the lateral boundaries are impervious above it. The base is fixed and
the lateral boundaries are on rollers.

\subsection{Discretization and solution}

Displacements are interpolated by quadratic and pressures by linear triangles (Taylor--Hood), in plane strain. The
storage terms are lumped at the vertices: the change of saturation as $n(S_w^{k+1}-S_w^k)$ and the compressibility
of the air as $nS_a^{k+1}(p_a^{k+1}-p_a^k)/(p_a^{k+1}+p_{atm})$, where $k$ is the time level. The equations are
integrated by the implicit Euler method and solved by Newton's method with the complete Jacobian, including the
derivatives of the permeabilities, of the saturation and of $\chi$. \citet[p.~1032]{bergamaschi1999} relate the
stagnation of the Picard method in Richards' equation to ``the absence of the derivative terms in the Jacobian
matrix''. Dirichlet conditions are eliminated and the linear systems are solved with Pardiso. A time step that does
not converge is restarted from its initial state with up to five levels of bisection. The time step is 15~min during
the rainfall, 1~h in the following 24~h and 4~h afterwards.

The global mass balances of water and air were checked in model (iv) with 36~mm/h, with the volume of each fluid
obtained from the lumped vertex values and the fluxes through the ground surface and the boundaries with prescribed
pressure. The water balance closes within 0.05\% of the infiltrated volume during the 48~h. The air balance closes
within 0.25\% of the initial air volume at the end of the rainfall and 0.34\% after 48~h, i.e.\ 0.9--1.1\% of the air
expelled through the ground surface.

\subsection{Factor of safety}\label{sec:fs}

At selected instants an independent stability analysis is made with $p_w$ and $p_a$ frozen: from the geostatic state
with the strength increased ($c'/F_0$, $\tan\phi'/F_0$ with $F_0=0.6$) the strength reduction factor $F$ is increased
until the equilibrium no longer converges; the FS is the last converged $F$, ``the ratio of the shear strength
parameters of soil to those at failure'' \citep[p.~4]{pradhan2022}, as in \citet{griffiths1999}. It is found by
bisection with a tolerance of 0.002 in the cases near the critical intensity and 0.01 in the others. The state of
the transient is restored afterwards. The strength reduction is always made with $\psi=\phi'$, also for the
transients computed with $\psi<\phi'$.

The transient is solved with the real strength in the coupled models: while it converges, a state in equilibrium
with the real strength and with the pressures of that instant exists. In all the coupled cases with $\psi=\phi'$
computed here, the strength reduction, which starts from the same pressures, gave a minimum FS of 1.000 or more
(Section~\ref{sec:decomp}). The loss of equilibrium with the real strength
(the transient does not converge even with five bisections) is recorded as the time $t_f$; the transient then
continues with the strength increased ($F_0$), only to follow the pore pressures. In the uncoupled models the
deformation does not change the pressures, the transient mechanics is solved with the strength increased ($F=0.25$)
and the FS of the strength reduction can fall below one.

\section{Verification}\label{sec:valid}

\subsection{Drainage of a sand column (Liakopoulos test)}

In the test of \citet{liakopoulos1965} a 1-m column of Del Monte sand, initially saturated with $p_w=0$, drains
through its base. The test has been ``considered by several authors to validate their numerical formulations''
\citep[p.~490]{callari2009}. The parameters are those of \citet[Table~1 and Eq.~58]{callari2009}: $E=1.3$~MPa,
$\nu=0.4$, $n=0.2975$, permeability $4.5\times10^{-7}$~m$^2$/(kPa\,s) ($k_s=4.41\times10^{-6}$~m/s),
$S_w=1-1.9722\times10^{-11}p_c^{2.4279}$ ($p_c$ in Pa) and $k_{rw}=1-2.207(1-S_w)^{1.0121}$; $\chi=S_w$, elastic
soil. For the air, which \citet[p.~490]{callari2009} do not model (``A constant atmospheric value is assumed for the
gas pressure''), we assume the Brooks--Corey permeability with $\lambda=3$ and a residual saturation of 0.2. The air
pressure is atmospheric at both ends, the water pressure at the base, and the top is impervious to water. The column
was discretized with $2\times80$ cells of two triangles and time steps of 30~s.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{liakopoulos.pdf}
\caption{Drainage of the Liakopoulos column. (a) Water and (b) air pressure profiles of the two-phase model. (c)
Water pressure of the passive-air model (solid) and of the two-phase model (dashed) and the measured values (squares,
digitized from \citealp[Fig.~3b]{callari2009}). (d) Outflow at the base and the measured outflow (digitized from
\citealp[Fig.~3a]{callari2009}).}\label{fig:liak}
\end{figure}

The passive-air solution reproduces the measurements (Fig.~\ref{fig:liak}c,d): the outflow differs from the measured
one by $0.29\times10^{-6}$~m/s in root mean square (maximum $0.97\times10^{-6}$~m/s, in the first minutes) and the
water pressures by 0.37--0.52~kPa in root mean square at 600, 1200 and 1800~s. The two-phase solution, with the
assumed air permeability, drains more slowly than the test: its outflow is 2.0--2.7 times smaller than that of the
passive-air solution between 10 and 30~min and the water pressure at 600~s differs from the measurements by 2.0~kPa
in root mean square. The test therefore verifies the implementation with passive air; it does not validate the air
phase, whose effect in drainage depends on an air permeability that the test does not measure. This agrees with
\citet[p.~490]{callari2009}, for whom the zero gas pressure ``is acceptable in many situations of interest and avoids
problems encountered with three-phase formulations in simulating the transition from full to partial saturation''.

and the base 20~m below the toe; the initial water table is 5~m below the toe and inclined at $7^\circ$, and the
initial pore-water pressure is hydrostatic ``with a limiting pore-water pressure of $-75$~kPa''
\citep[p.~1535]{rahardjo2007} (Table~\ref{tab:par}, Fig.~\ref{fig:problema}). The standard mesh has 1684 triangles,
with elements of 0.75~m down to 2~m below the surface.

The initial FS is 2.119; the regression of \citet[Table~7]{rahardjo2007} gives 2.118 at $45^\circ$, and for heights

never below it; the largest differences are in the transition between the upper and the lower plateau. The

by \citet[Tables~4 and 5]{zhang2014}, the three linear ones ($\phi^b=0$, $15^\circ$ and $\phi'$) are reproduced in
their steep and flat slopes within 0.05--3.9\%.

\section{Failure criterion and critical intensity}\label{sec:criterio}
%======================================================================

The slope fails when the FS reaches one. The FS of each instant is computed with the pressures of that instant
frozen; a value below one indicates that the available strength is insufficient for the analysed configuration and
that equilibrium with that strength is not possible. It does not describe the subsequent deformation or the
redistribution of the pore pressures, which the frozen-pressure analysis does not compute. The quantity compared is
therefore whether, with which rainfall and at which instant, the FS reaches one.

Because the minimum FS of the coupled models with an associated flow rule did not fall below one in any case
computed (Section~\ref{sec:fs}), the critical intensity is defined with a threshold $1+\delta$: $\Icd$ is the smallest
intensity of a 24-h rainfall whose minimum FS is $\le1+\delta$, obtained by linear interpolation between the computed
intensities that bracket it, and the time to failure is the first instant with FS $\le1+\delta$ (or $t_f$, if the
coupled transient loses equilibrium earlier). The values are given for $\delta=0$, 0.005, 0.01 and 0.02; $\delta$ must
exceed the bisection tolerance (0.002) to be meaningful in the coupled associated models, and the mesh error of the
minimum FS is 0.003--0.023 (Section~\ref{sec:malha}). Unless stated otherwise, $\Ic$ denotes $I_{c,0.01}$.

\citet[p.~1539]{rahardjo2007} call threshold rainfall intensity $I_{r(\mathrm{thr})}$ the intensity beyond which the
minimum FS ``remains constant''; it is not the intensity that brings the FS to one, and for the soil $f_{50}$ it is
51~mm/h ($1.42k_s$, their Fig.~7b). The lower FS plateau of the intense rainfalls is, in model (iii), the FS of the
slope saturated up to its surface with the water flowing through it. A steady-state calculation with the surface
ponded gives 0.84--0.85 for the three soils; in the transient with the surface ponded it is reached between 6 and
12~h, 12 and 24~h, and 96 and 192~h of ponding in the $f_{10}$, $f_{50}$ and $f_{100}$ soils (the instants computed).
It depends on the orientation of an anisotropic conductivity tensor (0.63--1.05 for $k_1/k_2=10$), not on the
magnitude of $k_s$. This agrees with \citet[p.~9]{li2024}, for whom the FS of a slope saturated by a steady
infiltration ``is identical to that without considering suction'', and with \citet[p.~17]{cheng2024}, who attribute
the constant minimum FS above a certain intensity to the degree of saturation, which ``keeps constant when the
rainfall intensity exceeds a certain value''. The lower asymptotes of \citet[Fig.~7]{rahardjo2007} are 0.81 and 0.82
for the $f_{10}$ and $f_{50}$ soils and 1.45 for the $f_{100}$ soil; with 24-h rainfalls the $f_{100}$ slope does not
reach the saturated state in model (iii) either, so its plateau is not the FS of the saturated slope. These
statements concern model (iii) and the reference slope.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:lim}
%======================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item Two-dimensional, homogeneous slope with a single geometry for the decomposition; the critical intensity of
other slopes was examined only for two soils, two Bishop laws and one steeper slope.
\item The air leaves only through the ground surface, and once it ponds only through the minimum relative
permeability $k_{ra}^{\min}$. Opening the base and the lateral boundaries does not change the results
(Section~\ref{sec:confinamento}); with $k_{ra}^{\min}=10^{-2}$ the minimum FS of the $f_{100}$ slope rises by 0.03.
Macropores, cracks, vegetation and heterogeneities are represented only through $k_{ra}^{\min}$.
\item The strength reduction is associated even when the transient uses $\psi=0$; the loss of equilibrium of the
transient is then the failure indicator, and part of it may be numerical.
\item No hysteresis of the retention curve, constant porosity in the hydraulic coefficients, no dissolved air, no
vapour and an isothermal ideal gas. The air compressibility is integrated with the saturation of the end of the step,
which is not exactly mass conservative for the air (Section~\ref{sec:form}).
\item The mesh error of the minimum FS was assessed in three cases (Fig.~\ref{fig:malha}): with 0.35-m elements it
falls by 0.006--0.023 and, in the $f_{100}$ case, by about 0.003 per refinement without clear convergence; the
critical intensities shift by 1--2~mm/h.
\item The air phase was verified only through the mass balance and was not validated: in the Liakopoulos test the
two-phase solution drains more slowly than the measurements, and no measurement of the air pressure under
infiltration was used.
\item The pressure profiles of Fig.~\ref{fig:ar}c,d were computed from snapshots that are not part of the published
data set; the other results are.
\end{itemize}
