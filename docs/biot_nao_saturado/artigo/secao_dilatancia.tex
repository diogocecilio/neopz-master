% Seção 7: dilatância e resposta depois da ruptura
The dilatancy does not decide whether a critical intensity exists, but it moves it and it decides what happens
afterwards (Figs.~\ref{fig:fstempo} and \ref{fig:decomp}). Without dilatancy ($\psi=0$) the coupled transient loses
equilibrium for $I_r\ge26\mmh$, between 21.5~h (26~mm/h) and 11.75~h (180~mm/h); with 22~mm/h it does not. With
$\psi=\phi'/2$ or $\psi=\phi'$ the FS stops at 1.000--1.008 and the transient never loses equilibrium: the slope sits in
limit equilibrium, with plastic deformation and the suction generated by the plastic dilation of the shear zone. Half
of the associated dilatancy is enough for this behaviour. Below the critical intensity the flow rule hardly matters:
with 18~mm/h the minimum FS is 1.316 with $\psi=0$ and 1.319 with $\psi=\phi'/2$ and $\phi'$. The dilatancy shifts
$\Ic$ by about 20\% (from 26 to 31~mm/h) and brings the failure forward by about 6~h in the intense rainfalls
(Table~\ref{tab:decomp}).

With $\psi=0$ the loss of equilibrium occurs when the FS of the strength reduction, computed with $\psi=\phi'$, is
still 1.05--1.06. Either the associated strength reduction overestimates by about 5\% the FS of a soil without
dilatancy, or part of the loss of equilibrium is numerical. The time step does not explain it:
with a time step of 0.125~h instead of 0.25~h during the rainfall, the transient with 36~mm/h loses equilibrium after
15.6~h instead of 15.5~h. For $\psi=0$ the time of failure is therefore $t_f$ and $22<\Ic\le26\mmh$; the FS values
below one after $t_f$ use the pressures of a transient continued with the strength increased and only show that the
equilibrium was exceeded.

The flow rule matters as much without active air. In model (ii), coupled with passive air, the transient without
dilatancy loses equilibrium after 22.3~h with 54~mm/h and the critical intensity is 53~mm/h; with $\psi=\phi'$ the
minimum FS stays between 1.010 and 1.033 from 54 to 78~mm/h and the transient never loses equilibrium. Dilatancy,
through the suction it generates in the shear zone, is the main reason why the coupled models with an associated
flow rule do not predict a collapse, and the reason why the plateau of the coupled models is close to one instead of
the 0.84--0.85 of the saturated slope.
