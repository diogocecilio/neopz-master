Physically based rainfall thresholds for unsaturated slopes are often derived from a seepage analysis with atmospheric
pore air followed by a stability analysis with the computed pore-water pressures. We quantify how these hypotheses
change the rainfall that triggers failure. A formulation with displacement, pore-water and pore-air pressures
(Bishop's effective stress, Mohr--Coulomb plasticity) is implemented in the open-source library NeoPZ and verified
against the Liakopoulos drainage test with passive air; the factor of safety (FS) is obtained by strength reduction
with frozen pressures. Because the coupled transient is solved with the real strength, the minimum FS of the coupled
models with an associated flow rule did not fall below one in the cases computed; models are therefore compared by the critical intensity
$I_{c,\delta}$ of a 24-h rainfall, the smallest that brings the minimum FS to $1+\delta$, and by the time to failure.
For the slope of Rahardjo et al.\ (2007) with $k_s=36$~mm/h and $\delta=0.005$--0.02, the uncoupled, passive-air model
gives $I_{c,\delta}=47$--49~mm/h ($1.3k_s$); the coupled model with active air gives 30--35~mm/h with an associated
flow rule and 26~mm/h without dilatancy. The usual hypotheses thus overestimate the failure-triggering rainfall by
40--59\% and 85--88\%, respectively, and delay the predicted failure by 4.5 and 9.9~h with 54~mm/h. Trapped air
halves $I_{c,\delta}$; coupling raises it, slightly without dilatancy and strongly with an associated flow rule. With
active air less water infiltrates and the matric suction is higher, but the pore-water pressure rises with the air
pressure; a low-permeability slope that the usual model keeps 9--10\% above failure comes within 0.009--0.015 of it.
Without dilatancy the coupled transient collapses; with $\psi\ge\phi'/2$ it does not. The results contradict the
assumption that atmospheric pore air is approximately true in situ once the surface ponds, and qualify published
statements on the adequacy of passive-air and uncoupled analyses.
