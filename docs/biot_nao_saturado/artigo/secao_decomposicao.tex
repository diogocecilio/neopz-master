Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} and Table~\ref{tab:decomp} give the minimum FS under 24-h rainfalls of the $f_{50}$ soil
($k_s=36\mmh$) in the four models, the critical intensities $\Icd$ and the times to failure. The usual model (iii)
gives $\Icd=47$--49~mm/h ($1.3k_s$) for $\delta=0$--0.02. The complete model (i) gives 30, 31 and 35~mm/h with
$\psi=\phi'$ for $\delta=0.02$, 0.01 and 0.005, and 26~mm/h with $\psi=0$ for all $\delta$; with $\psi=\phi'$ and
$\delta=0$ it has no critical intensity, its minimum FS being 1.000--1.003 from 36 to 180~mm/h. For
$\delta=0.005$--0.02 the hypotheses of the usual chain therefore overestimate the rainfall that brings the slope to
failure by 40--59\% with $\psi=\phi'$ and by 85--88\% with $\psi=0$. The sigmoid of \citet[Fig.~7b]{rahardjo2007}
reaches $1+\delta$ at 38--40~mm/h: model (iii) is 23--24\% above it, model (i) 12--22\% below it with $\psi=\phi'$
and 33--34\% below it with $\psi=0$. The usual hypotheses also delay the failure: with 54~mm/h the FS of model (iii)
reaches 1.01 after 22.4~h, against 17.9~h in model (i) with $\psi=\phi'$ and the loss of equilibrium after 12.5~h with
$\psi=0$; with 90~mm/h, after 19.7, 18.3 and 12.0~h. In these cases the FS was computed every 3~h after the third
hour, and the times are interpolated between the outputs.

The four models separate the two hypotheses (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The air lowers the critical intensity by a factor of 2.0--2.5}, for the same coupling and flow rule: on a
rigid skeleton from 47--49 to 24--25~mm/h (models iii and iv, $\delta=0$--0.02); in the coupled models from at most
52--53 to 26~mm/h without dilatancy (models ii and i; the run of model ii with 54~mm/h stopped at 24~h, after its loss
of equilibrium at 22.3~h) and from 62 to 30~mm/h ($\delta=0.02$) and from 78 to 31~mm/h ($\delta=0.01$) with
$\psi=\phi'$.
\item \emph{The coupling raises it}: without dilatancy by 5--7\% with active air (from 24--25 to 26~mm/h) and by at
most 11\% with passive air (from 47--48 to at most 52--53~mm/h); with $\psi=\phi'$ by 22--41\% with active air and by 32\% ($\delta=0.02$)
to 62\% ($\delta=0.01$) with passive air; with $\delta=0.005$ the critical intensity of model (ii) exceeds 90~mm/h.
Models (iii) and (ii), and models (iv) and (i), differ only by the volumetric strain terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:massa};
the mechanism of the increase (elastic or plastic volume change) was not separated.
\end{itemize}
The two simplifications of the usual model thus have opposite effects, and the effect of the air is the larger one.
A two-phase flow analysis on a rigid skeleton, model (iv), gives a critical intensity 5--6\% below that of the complete
model without dilatancy and 18--29\% below that with $\psi=\phi'$, and its time to failure with 36~mm/h, 16.6~h, lies
between those of the complete model with $\psi=0$ (15.5~h) and with $\psi=\phi'$ (21.3~h).

The coupled models with an associated flow rule approach one from above (1.000--1.014 for $I_r\ge I_{c,0.02}$), so
their critical intensity depends on $\delta$: from $\delta=0.02$ to 0.005 that of model (i) rises from 30 to 35~mm/h
and that of model (ii) from 62 to more than 90~mm/h, while those of the uncoupled models and of the models without
dilatancy change by less than 1.5~mm/h. In model (ii) with $\psi=\phi'$ the minimum FS is 1.009--1.014 from 66 to
90~mm/h: the slope is in limit equilibrium, within the resolution of the FS, over a wide range of intensities, with
plastic deformation but without loss of equilibrium.
