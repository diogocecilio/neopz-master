% Seção 12: conclusões
A coupled formulation with displacement, pore-water and pore-air pressures was implemented in NeoPZ, verified against
the Liakopoulos drainage test with passive air and used to quantify how the hypotheses of the usual chain of
analysis, seepage on a rigid skeleton with atmospheric pore air followed by a stability analysis with the computed
pressures, change the rainfall that brings an unsaturated slope to failure. The conclusions refer to the slope of
\citet{rahardjo2007} and the cases computed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item In the coupled models with an associated flow rule the transient is solved with the real strength, and the
minimum FS of the strength reduction did not fall below one in the cases computed (1.000--1.014 for $I_r\ge\Ic$). The models must
therefore be compared by the critical intensity $\Icd$ for a stated threshold $1+\delta$ and by the time to failure,
not by the minimum FS; with $\delta=0$ the complete associated model has no critical intensity up to 180~mm/h.
\item For $k_s=36\mmh$ and 24-h rainfalls the usual model (iii) gives $\Icd=47$--49~mm/h ($1.3k_s$) and the complete
model (i) 30--35~mm/h with $\psi=\phi'$ and 26~mm/h with $\psi=0$ ($\delta=0.005$--0.02). The usual hypotheses
overestimate the failure-triggering rainfall by 40--59\% with $\psi=\phi'$ and by 85--88\% with $\psi=0$, and delay
the predicted failure by 4.5~h ($\psi=\phi'$) and 9.9~h ($\psi=0$) with 54~mm/h.
\item The two simplifications act in opposite directions. With the same coupling, active air lowers $\Icd$ by a
factor of 2.0--2.5 (from 48 to 25~mm/h on a rigid skeleton, $\delta=0.01$); the coupling raises it, by
5--11\% without dilatancy (at most 11\% with passive air) and by 27--62\% with $\psi=\phi'$ ($\delta=0.01$). The air effect is the larger
one, and a two-phase flow analysis on a rigid skeleton (model iv) gives a critical intensity below that of the
complete model.
\item With active air, less water infiltrates and the matric suction below the face is higher than with passive air,
but the pore-water pressure is also higher and the FS lower. On the low-permeability slope at $63.4^\circ$
($k_s=3.6\mmh$), which model (iii) keeps 9--10\% above failure, the complete model gives a minimum FS of 1.013--1.015
(1.009 with the finest mesh). Opening the base and the lateral boundaries to the air does not change these results.
\item Without dilatancy the coupled transient loses equilibrium for $I_r\ge26\mmh$; with $\psi\ge\phi'/2$ it does not
in the range computed. Whether a coupled analysis predicts a collapse therefore depends on the flow rule.
\item The bias persists with $\chi=S_e$ (64--77\%) and with a finer mesh (about 46 against 30~mm/h). In the
permeable $f_{10}$ soil it changes sign: the usual model gives a critical intensity 2--6\% below that of the complete
model, although the surface ponds and the air pressure rises; the reason was not established.
\end{enumerate}
Several statements of the literature are questioned or qualified by these results (Section~\ref{sec:lit}): in our
analyses the pore air is far from atmospheric once the surface ponds, whereas \citet{sheng2008} consider the
atmospheric value ``approximately true for most in situ conditions''; neither the passive-air assumption nor the
uncoupled solution is generally adequate or inadequate, the answer depending on the process and on the flow rule; and
the threshold intensity relative to $k_s$ is not ``about or slightly larger than 1'' in the study that
\citet{liu2021} cite for it. The validation of the air phase against measured air pressures under infiltration, natural (open, heterogeneous)
slopes and three-dimensional effects are the next steps.
