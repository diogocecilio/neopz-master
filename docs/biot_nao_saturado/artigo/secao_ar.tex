% Seção 6: ar aprisionado (grupo G1 do item A2, f100, alpha = 63,4 graus; confinamento, k_ra,min, malha)
\citet{rahardjo2007} classified the slopes with $k_s\le10^{-6}$~m/s as safe in rainfalls of up to 24~h, whatever the
intensity, because the rain does not raise the water table. We examine the $f_{100}$ soil ($k_s=3.6\mmh$) on the
steepest slope of their study, $\alpha=63.4^\circ$, whose initial FS is 1.775 (Fig.~\ref{fig:ar}). With the usual
model (iii) the minimum FS under 24-h rainfalls is 1.38, 1.16, 1.10 and 1.09 for $I_r=9$, 18, 36 and 80~mm/h: the
slope does not fail, as the classification states. With model (ii) the FS is 0.02--0.04 higher. With active air it is
1.17, 1.03, 1.02 and 1.01: from $I_r=5k_s$ on, the slope reaches limit equilibrium at the end of the rainfall.

The mechanism is not more water. With active air 10.4--11.6~m$^3$/m infiltrate in 24~h, against 13.5--17.9~m$^3$/m
with passive air (Fig.~\ref{fig:ar}b). The air resistance slows the infiltration and the surface ponds earlier: with
9~mm/h the first vertex ponds after 6~h with active air and after 11.75~h with passive air, and at the end of the
rainfall 83 and 41 of the 96 surface vertices are ponded; with 36~mm/h the whole surface ponds within 4.5~h. Once
the surface is sealed, the air between the wetting front and the water table can no longer escape and is compressed
by the water that still enters: with 36~mm/h $p_a$ reaches 19.5~kPa after 1~h, 30~kPa after 3~h and 38~kPa after
24~h, and averages 32~kPa between 0.5 and 5~m below the surface. The matric suction does not decrease, it increases:
between 2 and 5~m it is 38~kPa with active air and 24~kPa with passive air (Fig.~\ref{fig:ar}d), because less water
has arrived. What rises is the water pressure, pushed by the compressed air: $-6$ against $-24\kpa$ in the same
layer (Fig.~\ref{fig:ar}c). With $\chi=1$ the effective stress depends only on $p_w$, and the shear strength
decreases although the soil is drier. The usual rule that the stability depends on the infiltrated volume therefore
compares cases of the same model only; between models, less water gives a smaller FS.

The air pressure is controlled by how easily the air leaves through the ponded surface. With the minimum relative
permeability of the air $k_{ra}^{\min}$ between $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-3}$ the minimum FS (1.013--1.015) and the
maximum air pressure (37--38~kPa) do not change; with $10^{-2}$ the air escapes through the ponded surface, $p_a$
falls to 28~kPa and the FS rises to 1.05.

\subsection{Confinement of the air}\label{sec:confinamento}

The base and the lateral boundaries are closed to the air in the reference model, as in the other studies of rainfall
infiltration with the air phase. This is the upper bound of the entrapment through the boundaries. Opening them (atmospheric air pressure on the base
and on the lateral boundaries) changes the minimum FS by less than 0.002: 1.017 against 1.015 for the $f_{100}$ slope
with 36~mm/h, and 1.011 against 1.011 for the $f_{50}$ slope with 30~mm/h. The lateral boundaries are 30~m away from
the slope and the base is below the water table; the air is sealed by the ponded surface above it, not by the
boundaries of the domain.

\subsection{Mesh}\label{sec:malha}

The minimum FS of the $f_{100}$ slope with 36~mm/h decreases with the mesh: 1.0154, 1.0122 and 1.0090 with elements
of 0.75, 0.5 and 0.35~m near the surface (1764, 3814 and 7622 triangles), without a clear sign of convergence, while
the initial FS converges (1.775, 1.761, 1.752 and 1.748 with 0.25~m; Fig.~\ref{fig:malha}). The margin of 1.5\% of
the standard mesh is of the order of the discretization error, and by the criterion adopted the case is imminent
failure; whether the converged minimum FS falls below one would require finer meshes or a resolution of the shear
band.
