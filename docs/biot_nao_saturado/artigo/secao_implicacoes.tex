% Seção 10: consequências para limiares de chuva
For the reference slope and 24-h rainfalls, the critical intensity of the usual model is $1.3k_s$ and that of the
complete model 0.8--1.0$k_s$ with an associated flow rule and $0.7k_s$ without dilatancy ($\delta=0.005$--0.02).
A threshold derived from the usual chain is thus not conservative for this slope: rainfalls between the two
thresholds, which the usual analysis classifies as safe, bring the complete model to failure. On an
intensity--duration--frequency curve a lower intensity of the same duration has a shorter return period; by how much
depends on the local curve, which is not part of this study. \citet[p.~1541]{rahardjo2007} conclude that low
permeability prevents the failure under short rainfalls; for the $f_{100}$ slope at $63.4^\circ$ this holds in model
(iii) but, in model (i), only with $\delta\le0.01$ (Section~\ref{sec:ar}).

The bias is not uniform. It is large in the $f_{50}$ soil and on the $f_{100}$ slope at $63.4^\circ$, and small in
the $f_{10}$ soil, although the surface ponds and the air pressure rises in all three (Sections~\ref{sec:ar} and
\ref{sec:gen}); the reason for the difference was not established here. A two-phase flow analysis on a rigid skeleton
(model iv) gives a critical intensity 5--6\% below that of the complete model without dilatancy and
18--29\% below that with an associated flow rule; the usual single-phase seepage analysis gives one 85--88\% and
40--59\% above them.
